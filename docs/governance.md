# Governance proposal

**Status:** Discussion draft. This document does not establish a legal entity,
appoint a board, define an adopted standard, or start an election.

## Principles

The community should develop AI and agent specifications in public, using
open participation, transparent decisions, documented conflicts of interest,
and evidence of interoperability. The proposed board is accountable to
contributors and should not represent a single vendor or geographic region.
No project can declare itself an industry standard merely by adopting a
document; external uptake and independently verifiable conformance matter.

## Bootstrap

Until a community adopts an election charter, repository maintainers may
facilitate discussions and maintain the project, but they are not an elected
standards board. Publish this draft for review, recruit contributors and
nominees, and agree on identity verification, the electorate, election
administration, and a schedule before opening nominations. Do not hold an
election until there are enough eligible voters and candidates for a
meaningful, contested vote. If these conditions are not met, publish the
blocker and extend the bootstrap period rather than inventing participants.

## Proposed elected board

The following is a starting point for community review, not an adopted policy:

- Seven seats, elected by eligible contributors for two-year terms. Aim for
  staggered elections after the first term; define the transition before the
  first vote.
- A voter is a real person with at least two accepted, substantive contributions
  to the project in the preceding 12 months. Contributions can include code,
  specification reviews, documentation, accessibility, moderation, or
  reproducible testing. Establish and publish an auditable verification and
  appeals process before fixing the electoral roll.
- Eligible contributors may nominate themselves or accept a nomination.
  Candidates disclose relevant affiliations and conflicts. The community
  should agree on candidate eligibility and an organization-affiliation cap
  before nominations open.
- An independent election committee publishes the timeline, voter roll
  verification and appeals process, voting method, tie rules, results, and
  dispute process in advance. Use one person, one vote and a secret ballot;
  publish aggregate results and a verifiable process, not individual ballots.
- Vacancies, removal for cause, recusal, quorum, and term limits need explicit
  rules adopted before the election. Publish board decisions and rationales;
  handle confidential security or personal matters privately.

Identity and eligibility checks must collect the minimum necessary information,
document retention and deletion, and provide a way to challenge errors without
publishing personal data. No person, organization, or agent is deemed elected
by this proposal.

## Specification process

Anyone can propose a specification through a public, version-controlled
proposal containing the problem, scope, prior art, terminology, normative
requirements, security and privacy considerations, compatibility and migration
plans, and a testable conformance section. Authors disclose relevant
affiliations and intellectual-property concerns.

Proposed lifecycle:

1. **Draft:** author submits a proposal for public discussion and assigns a
   stable identifier.
2. **Review:** reviewers assess technical merit, accessibility, security,
   interoperability, prior art, and intellectual-property risks; objections
   and resolutions are recorded.
3. **Candidate:** publish a stable version, an explicit review deadline, and
   executable conformance tests. Seek independent implementations and
   document unresolved objections.
4. **Adopted:** an elected board votes under an adopted quorum and conflict
   policy after public review. Publish the decision, version, evidence, and
   any dissent. Adoption is a project decision, not a claim of external
   recognition or universal compliance.
5. **Maintenance:** publish errata, compatible revisions, deprecations, and
   withdrawal decisions with migration guidance and version history.

Before any adoption, the community must agree on review periods, required
independent implementations, voting thresholds, appeals, and the applicable
copyright, patent, and licensing policies. Existing standards bodies and
specifications should be engaged rather than duplicated where practical.

## Open decisions

The community must select open-source and specification licenses, an
intellectual-property policy, a legal home if needed, a code of conduct,
security reporting channels, election administration, and funding safeguards.
These decisions require public review and should precede claims of formal
standardization or a production launch.
